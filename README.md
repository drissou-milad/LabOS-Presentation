# LabOS

### AI-Assisted Biological Image Analysis and Cell Lineage Research

**LabOS** is an independent research software project exploring the use of artificial intelligence and computer vision to support biological image analysis, with an initial focus on cell tracking and lineage reconstruction from time-lapse microscopy data.

The project aims to help researchers analyse cellular behaviour, follow cell development over time, and extract structured information from microscopy experiments.

---

## The Scientific Problem

Analysing time-lapse microscopy data can be challenging. Researchers may encounter:

- Rapid cell movement between consecutive frames.
- Large inter-frame displacement.
- Weak or noisy image signals.
- Out-of-focus images and changes in image quality.
- Difficulties maintaining consistent cell identities.
- Complex cell division and lineage relationships.

These challenges can make reliable tracking and quantitative analysis difficult.

## The LabOS Approach

LabOS explores an integrated workflow for biological image analysis, combining computer vision, tracking algorithms and AI-assisted analysis.

The intended workflow includes:

1. Microscopy image input and preparation.
2. Cell detection and classification.
3. Cell tracking across consecutive frames.
4. Cell division event analysis.
5. Cell lineage reconstruction.
6. Quantitative analysis and research-oriented outputs.


## Current Development

The project has an initial technical implementation and ongoing experimental development.

Areas of work include:

- Cell tracking using assignment-based methods.
- Lineage reconstruction and division-related logic.
- Biological image dataset evaluation.
- Benchmarking and experimental validation.
- Development of an integrated research workflow.

Some AI-based detection components remain experimental. Their performance and scientific reliability require further evaluation.

## Research Direction

The initial application focuses on developmental biology and live-cell microscopy, including the analysis of developing zebrafish embryo data.

Potential future research directions include:

- More robust tracking under challenging imaging conditions.
- Improved cell division analysis.
- Cell lineage analytics.
- Investigation of cell behaviour and other biological events.
- Adaptation to additional microscopy research applications.

These are research directions, not claims of completed or clinically validated functionality.

## Project Status

LabOS is an independently developed student research software project currently undergoing technical development and validation.

It is not presented as a clinically validated diagnostic system or a production-ready medical product.
